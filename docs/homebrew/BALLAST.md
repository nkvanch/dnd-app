# Ballast

## Species Identity

Ballasts are a triumph of mass over mobility.

At first glance, a Ballast resembles a humanoid designed by someone who had heard the general idea of “a person” but was much more familiar with barrels, anchors, retaining walls, and objects labeled **DO NOT ATTEMPT TO LIFT WITHOUT MACHINERY**. They are broad, squat, thick-limbed, and astonishingly dense. A Medium Ballast may weigh several hundred kilograms. A Large one can weigh enough that introductions are occasionally followed by structural-engineering concerns.

Nobody is entirely sure why Ballasts are like this. The Ballasts themselves do not appear especially troubled by the question.

They move slowly, because acceleration is an ambitious project. They are difficult to push, drag, knock over, carry, float, throw, reposition, relocate, or politely ask to stand somewhere else. A Ballast who has decided to remain in one place has entered into a contractual relationship with that location.

Naturally, this has made Ballasts extremely popular in port cities.

Ships need ballast. Ballasts are ballast. The arrangement is so obvious that several coastal cultures independently invented the profession of “please stand over there until the weather improves.” Ballasts have historically served as dockworkers, living counterweights, emergency anchors, stabilizers, doorstops of last resort, and occasionally passengers whose ticket price included a reduction in required cargo ballast.

This does not mean Ballasts are especially strong. A Ballast can possess the upper-body strength of a wet librarian and still require six people, a block and tackle, and a written plan to move across a warehouse. Their defining characteristic is not muscle. It is the simple fact that there is **an unreasonable amount of Ballast occupying the place where the Ballast currently is**.

Water presents no philosophical difficulty either.

Ballasts sink.

They do not paddle ineffectively. They do not bob. They do not “struggle to stay afloat.” They proceed downward with confidence until the environment provides a floor.

Ballasts do not learn to swim.

They learn where the bottom is.

Their lifespan is unknown. This is not because Ballasts are immortal. It is because nobody has yet gathered sufficiently reliable demographic information, owing largely to the logistical problem of moving the gravestones.

## Species Traits

### Ability Scores

Increase one ability score by **2** and a different ability score by **1**.

### Size

Choose **Medium** or **Large**.

Large Ballasts use the normal consequences of being a Large creature already supported by the rules. This does not automatically grant extra reach, larger weapon dice, or other invented benefits.

### Typical Mass

A Ballast commonly weighs roughly **500–1,000 kg**, with substantial individual variation.

For effects involving another creature attempting to lift, drag, carry, throw, or otherwise physically relocate you, use your actual weight.

Your body mass does **not** increase your carrying capacity. Carrying capacity is still determined normally by your Strength and the rules being used at the table.

### Speed

Your walking speed is **15 feet**.

### Vision

Normal vision.

### Languages

You can speak, read, and write:

- Common
- one language of your choice

### Equipment

Equipment functions normally for you regardless of size unless the table chooses to care about equipment sizing.

## Catastrophically Dense

Your class Hit Die increases by one die size:

- d6 → d8
- d8 → d10
- d10 → d12

If a class already uses a **d12 Hit Die**, the Hit Die remains a d12 and your hit point maximum instead increases by **3 for each level you have in that class**.

This benefit is retroactive and applies to every level you have in the affected class.

For multiclass characters, resolve Catastrophically Dense separately for each class.

### Examples

- Wizard 6: d6 → d8
- Emperor Warlock 6: d8 → d10
- Fighter 6: d10 → d12
- Barbarian 6: d12 remains d12, +18 maximum HP
- Wizard 3 / Barbarian 3: Wizard Hit Dice become d8; Barbarian Hit Dice remain d12 and contribute +9 maximum HP

This trait affects both the class Hit Dice used for rest recovery and the corresponding class-based hit-point progression.

## No.

### Lesser / Demo Version

When an attack hits you, you can use your **reaction** to cause the attack to bounce away from you instead. The attack has no effect on you and continues in a random direction.

The new direction cannot pass through your space toward your back, because that would not be bouncing. That would simply be the attack phasing through you, and Ballasts consider this an entirely different and deeply suspicious phenomenon.

Once you use this trait, you cannot use it again until you finish a **Long Rest**.

### Original Joke Version

The original version does **not** consume your Reaction.

And “attack” means attack.

Sword? Bounce.

Arrow? Bounce.

Spell attack? Bounce.

Attack from something that clearly should not bounce? An excellent opportunity for the universe to learn something.

### Grimoire Representation

Structured:

- Reaction in the lesser/demo version
- 1 use
- Long Rest recharge
- defensive feature/action

Table-resolved:

- random ricochet direction
- what the attack hits afterward
- consequences of the ricochet

## Ballast

### Lesser / Demo Version

You have advantage on ability checks and saving throws made to resist being:

- shoved
- knocked prone
- dragged
- pushed
- pulled
- otherwise moved against your will

### Original Joke Version

You cannot be moved against your will.

No size comparison.

No contested roll.

No discussion.

The moving force has made a tactical error.

## Negative Buoyancy

You do not naturally float.

Unless supported by another creature, object, or magical effect, you sink in water.

If you reach a solid surface underwater, you can move along it using your walking speed, subject to whatever environmental rules the table is using.

Most consequences of this trait are table-resolved rather than automated.

> Swimming is what happens when something fails to sink correctly.

## Culture

Ballasts are a genuine people with their own communities, traditions, professions, and settlements. They are particularly common in port cities because every maritime civilization eventually makes the same discovery:

**Ballasts are spectacularly useful around ships.**

Their culture has largely accepted this fact.

Ballasts can be found as dockworkers, engineers, guards, sailors, merchants, mercenaries, living counterweights, emergency anchors, and people asked to “stand over there for a minute” during increasingly serious maritime emergencies.

They are not inherently stronger than other peoples. They are simply extremely difficult to make be somewhere else.

## Original vs. Lesser Version

### Ballast — Original

- Medium or Large
- absurd actual mass
- 15 ft walking speed
- Catastrophically Dense
- `No.` does not require a Reaction
- cannot be moved against their will
- Negative Buoyancy

### Ballast — Lesser / Demo

- Medium or Large
- same mass and speed
- same Catastrophically Dense mechanic
- `No.` costs a Reaction, 1/Long Rest
- advantage against forced movement rather than absolute immunity
- Negative Buoyancy unchanged
