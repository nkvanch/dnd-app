# Glassback

**Large aberration, unaligned**

A Glassback is a non-carbon extremophile native to deep, high-pressure cave systems saturated with mineral-rich brines, corrosive salts, and heavy ore deposits.

Its body is a low-slung six-legged pressure vessel of layered ceramic plates, dark flexible joints, crystalline sensory organs, and dense internal mineral slurry. It does not hunt flesh in the usual sense. It seeks metal, salts, crystal, and ore, and may attack a heavily armored creature while ignoring an unarmored one standing nearby.

Glassbacks are clever but nonverbal. They learn patterns, remember hazards, recognize dangerous creatures, and retreat when survival becomes unlikely.

## Traits

### Alien Metabolism

The Glassback does not breathe.

It does not require conventional food, but must periodically consume mineral-rich material such as ore, salts, crystal, or metal-bearing rock.

It cannot see normally. Its blindsight and tremorsense represent vibration, pressure, thermal-gradient, and chemical sensing.

### Mineral Appetite

The Glassback can detect significant exposed concentrations of metal, salt, crystal, or ore within 60 feet, provided they are not completely sealed behind a substantial barrier.

When choosing targets, a Glassback usually prioritizes the richest nearby mineral source unless another creature presents an immediate threat.

This is a behavioral tendency, not magical compulsion.

### Ceramic Shell

The Glassback reduces slashing damage it takes by **2** from each hit.

If the Glassback takes **10 or more bludgeoning or thunder damage from a single source**, one shell plate fractures until the end of its next turn.

While fractured:

- its AC is reduced by 1;
- a creature that hits it with a melee attack takes **3 (1d6) slashing damage** from razor-edged shell fragments.

Only one shell fracture can be active at a time.

### Pressure State

The Glassback has a Pressure value from **0 to 3**.

In its native high-pressure environment, it normally begins at **3 Pressure**.

In ordinary surface-pressure conditions, it normally begins at **2 Pressure**.

At the end of each of its turns while outside a high-pressure environment, it loses 1 Pressure, to a minimum of 0.

Pressure effects:

- **3 — Native Pressure:** no penalty.
- **2 — Stable:** no penalty.
- **1 — Strained:** AC reduced by 1 and all speeds reduced by 10 feet.
- **0 — Depressurized:** all speeds are halved, the Glassback cannot use Abrasive Jet, and it has disadvantage on Strength and Dexterity checks and saving throws.

Pressure penalties stack with any AC reduction from Ceramic Shell.

### Compress

As an action, the Glassback seals and compresses its body.

It regains **1 Pressure**, to a maximum of 3.

Until the start of its next turn:

- its speed becomes 0;
- it has resistance to bludgeoning, piercing, and slashing damage.

## Actions

### Multiattack

The Glassback makes **two attacks**, choosing from Grinding Mandibles and Forelimb Crush.

### Grinding Mandibles

**Melee Weapon Attack:** +7 to hit, reach 5 ft., one target.  
**Hit:** 15 (2d10 + 4) piercing damage.

Against an object made primarily of metal, stone, crystal, or another mineral material, the attack deals an additional **7 (2d6) damage**.

### Forelimb Crush

**Melee Weapon Attack:** +7 to hit, reach 10 ft., one target.  
**Hit:** 13 (2d8 + 4) bludgeoning damage.

If the target is Medium or smaller, it must succeed on a **DC 15 Strength saving throw** or be pushed 5 feet and knocked prone.

### Abrasive Jet (Recharge 5–6)

The Glassback expels a high-pressure stream of mineral slurry in a **30-foot-long, 5-foot-wide line**.

Each creature in the line must make a **DC 15 Dexterity saving throw**.

On a failed save, a creature takes:

- **18 (4d8) slashing damage**
- **7 (2d6) acid damage**

and its speed is reduced by 10 feet until the end of its next turn.

On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and suffers no speed reduction.

The slurry rapidly crystallizes. The affected line becomes **difficult terrain until the end of the Glassback's next turn**.

The Glassback cannot use Abrasive Jet while at 0 Pressure.

## Death Trait

### Pressure Collapse

When the Glassback is reduced to 0 hit points, its internal pressure catastrophically equalizes.

Each creature within 10 feet must make a **DC 15 Dexterity saving throw**.

On a failed save, the creature takes **9 (2d8) slashing damage** and is pushed 5 feet away from the Glassback.

On a successful save, it takes half as much damage and is not pushed.

Afterward, the Glassback's body becomes a hollow mass of brittle ceramic plates, crystallized mineral fluid, and dense internal deposits.

## Combat Behavior

A Glassback does not fight like a conventional predator.

Typical priorities:

1. Seek exposed metal, salt, crystal, or ore.
2. Attack heavily equipped creatures before lightly equipped ones if the threat level is otherwise similar.
3. Use Abrasive Jet when multiple targets or valuable mineral-bearing objects line up.
4. Use Forelimb Crush to create space when surrounded.
5. At low Pressure, use Compress or retreat toward denser terrain.
6. When badly injured, burrow toward deeper, higher-pressure ground rather than fight to the death.

A Glassback may ignore a motionless, lightly equipped creature if a richer mineral source is available nearby.

---

## Ecology

Glassbacks evolved in deep cave systems where extreme pressure, corrosive brines, mineral saturation, and low oxygen make ordinary carbon-based life rare.

They excavate tunnels by grinding and chemically weakening mineral deposits, leaving behind unnaturally smooth channels and scored stone.

Surface encounters usually happen because a Glassback follows a rich ore vein upward, is displaced by cave collapse, or is brought toward lower pressure by mining activity.

At ordinary surface pressure, its body slowly destabilizes, making prolonged exposure stressful even when the creature is not immediately injured.
